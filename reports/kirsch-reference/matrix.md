# Derived exact-restriction Kirsch reference

The [prospectively committed specification](../../studies/kirsch_reference/spec.json) defines a quarter-square plane-stress domain outside a circular hole. Remote positive tension acts along +y; angle is measured from +x. The [OpenGeoSys Kirsch page](https://www.opengeosys.org/6.5.7/docs/benchmarks/small-deformations/kirsch/) supplies the infinite-plane polar stress equations and this loading convention. Our finite-domain problem is the exact restriction of that field. Its right and top artificial boundaries receive the complete position-dependent traction vector, including shear. Uniform tension on a finite edge would define a different approximate boundary-value problem. The hole is traction-free. The left and bottom symmetry edges constrain their normal displacements, respectively, excluding in-plane rigid motions together. The claim is circumferential stress at the hole's +x point, in MPa, under the stated ideal linear-elastic plane-stress model. No physical strength or application acceptance decision is made.

The [executed result](results.json) is the sole numeric output source. The usual polar stress formula and a separately derived Airy scalar potential are evaluated by different code routes. With remote y-stress S, hole radius a, radius r and angle θ, the Airy potential is

`Φ = S/4 [r² - 2a² ln(r/a) + (r² - 2a² + a⁴/r²) cos(2θ)]`.

The potential follows from the biharmonic basis `r²`, `ln r`, `r² cos(2θ)`, `cos(2θ)` and `r⁻² cos(2θ)`: remote uniaxial stress fixes the growing terms, while zero radial and shear traction at `r=a` fixes the remaining coefficients. This route starts from the potential and boundary conditions rather than transforming the polar stress array. Cartesian second derivatives give `σxx=Φyy`, `σyy=Φxx`, and `σxy=-Φxy`. A second-order Cartesian jet computes these derivatives without calling or transforming the polar stress implementation. The potential gives the standard remote-y Kirsch field and is biharmonic outside the hole. Modulus and Poisson ratio affect the compatible displacement field, but the prescribed-traction stress field is independent of them in this linear model. Numerical divergence checks test local equilibrium. Under plane stress and equilibrium, in-plane strain compatibility reduces to harmonicity of `σxx+σyy`; the registered finite-difference Laplacian tests that condition. The [source code](../../studies/kirsch_reference/reference.py) and [runner](../../studies/kirsch_reference/run.py) also check traction-free hole and symmetry shear, the full outer traction, and force and moment closure over all five oriented boundary segments. A far-field limit and zero-load control exercise separate limiting behavior. The Airy calculation is separately derived, but both routes represent the same analytical elasticity solution; neither is an FE convergence study or physical measurement.
